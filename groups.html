Research & Collaboration Groups

Meetups are where you meet people. Groups are where the work happens: three or more members, one shared question, a regular slot, and something concrete at the end.

Start a groupSee active groups

How they work

Small, self-organising, and accountable to nobody but themselves

A group is whatever its members need it to be — a reading group, a joint proposal team, a prototype squad, a benchmark effort. The community's only asks are that it stays open to new members, that it meets on a predictable schedule, and that it reports back at a meetup once a term so the rest of us know what it is up to.

  • Three people minimum, ideally from more than one department.
  • One convener who books the room and sends the reminder. That is the whole job.
  • A cadence you can keep — every two weeks or monthly beats an ambitious weekly slot that quietly dies.
  • An output in mind: a paper, a proposal, a benchmark, a tool, a course module, a report. Even a well-written negative result counts.
  • Open doors. Anyone from the community can join or sit in.

What the community provides

  • A slot at a meetup to recruit members and present progress.
  • A page on this site, plus a repository in the community GitHub organisation.
  • Help finding collaborators with the skills you are missing.
  • A track at the quarterly symposium to publish your results internally.

Groups are not a commitment device

If a group has run its course, say so and close it. Ending cleanly is better than a calendar invitation that everyone quietly declines.

Proposed themes

Six directions to start from

These are suggestions, not a fixed list — a starting menu drawn from the interests we expect across campus. Claim one, merge two, or propose something entirely different.

Agents for Scientific Discovery

Literature sweeps, hypothesis generation, simulation campaigns, and lab automation. Can an agent run a credible, reproducible experimental loop — and how would we know?

Industrial & Energy Operations

Agents over sensor data, maintenance records, and operating procedures. Reliability, cost, and the approval boundary between an agent's plan and a human decision.

Agent Engineering & Infrastructure

The unglamorous half: orchestration frameworks, tool interfaces, retrieval, memory, observability, cost control, and running all of it on real infrastructure.

Evaluation, Safety & Governance

How do you measure whether an agent works? Benchmarks, failure taxonomies, red-teaming, and policy for agent use in research, teaching and university operations.

Agents in Education

Tutors that guide rather than answer, agent-assisted labs, and the harder question of what assessment and academic integrity mean now.

Arabic & Domain-Specific Agents

Performance in Arabic and in specialist technical domains: data, evaluation sets, terminology, and the gaps general-purpose systems leave behind.

Nothing here fits your question? Good — that is a new group.

Start your own

Four steps from idea to first meeting

The bar is intentionally low. Most groups here start with one person saying out loud at a meetup that they want to understand something better.

Tell us your idea

  • Step 1Write three sentences

    The question, why it matters, and what a good outcome would look like six months from now.

  • Step 2Pitch it in two minutes

    At the next meetup's open floor, or by email if you would rather not present. We will circulate it.

  • Step 3Find two others

    We help with introductions. Two committed people beats ten interested ones.

  • Step 4Pick a slot and start

    We add the group to this site, set up a repository, and put you on the symposium programme.
